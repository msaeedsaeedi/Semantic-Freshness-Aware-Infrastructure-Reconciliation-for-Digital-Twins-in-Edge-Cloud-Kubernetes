1Problem

  • A Digital Twin that drives orchestration can turn every telemetry change into an action such as a pod migration, which costs bandwidth, energy and compute.
  • Event-driven designs churn on noise bursts; periodic designs are cheap but slow to follow real changes.
When is a deviation large and lasting enough to act on?

2Research question

Under dynamic edge-cloud workloads, can a persistence-based reconciliation policy reduce unnecessary infrastructure updates while following the sustained demand at least as well as a periodic policy of equal cost?

3The persistence rule

Sample xs(k) every Δ = %%DELTA%% ticks; rs = state at the last reconciliation.

ns(k) = ( ns(k−1) + 1 ) · 1[ |xs(k) − rs| > θ ]
reconcile when ns(k) ≥ c; then rs ← xs(k), ns ← 0
  • Proposed: θ = %%THETA%%, c = %%C%% on raw telemetry.
  • Deviations shorter than %%CMIN%% ticks cannot trigger an update.
  • A persistent shift is acted on after %%DLO%%–%%DHI%% ticks (ideal bound).
Not a new algorithm: a debounce / hysteresis rule (cf. the Kubernetes HPA stabilization window). Our contribution is a controlled evaluation with injected ground truth.

10Takeaways

  • Persistence filters short spikes without a timer: better tracking and faster reaction than periodic at equal cost.
  • A trade-off, not a free win: event-driven reacts fastest but needs %%REC%%% more reconciliations.
  • Age of Information is fixed by sampling (%%AOI%% ticks for every policy), so it cannot rank these policies.

11Limitations

  • Synthetic workloads only; no real Kubernetes cluster (cost = reconciliations).
  • Spikes (+10) exceed real jumps (±8), so amplitude alone cannot separate them; the advantage may shrink when spikes are smaller than real shifts.
  • Phase 2: 3 of 6 servers, greedy migration, %%P2SEEDS%% seeds.
  • No comparison with tuned HPA or CUSUM/EWMA detectors.
